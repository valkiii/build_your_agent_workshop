Do they need to set up beforehand?

Short answer: yes, the download has to happen before the day — but your instinct about suspense is right, and you lose none of it.

The download and the payoff are separable, and the repo already separates them. setup.command / setup.bat installs everything and then deliberately stops — it never opens the app. Participants arrive with 9 GB on disk and no idea what it does.

The reason it can't wait is arithmetic, not caution. The model alone is ~7.2 GB, pulled from Ollama's registry, plus the Ollama installer, the Python dependencies and the ~340 MB text-to-speech voice. Call it 9 GB each. Twenty-five people starting that simultaneously on venue wifi is a couple of hundred gigabytes of contention — you would lose the first hour and some would still be stuck at 10:00.

The invite email

Written to survive skimming: the one mandatory action is boxed and impossible to miss, and the "you don't need to code" reassurance comes before it, so non-coders don't bail at the sight of a setup step.

Paste into the calendar invite

SubjectAgents at Home, [DATE] — one thing to do before you arrive

Hi all,

On [DATE], 9:00–12:20 at [LOCATION], we're spending the morning building an AI agent that runs entirely on your own laptop. No cloud, no API keys, no accounts, nothing leaving your machine.

What we build is a Distraction-Free Content Curator. It goes out and reads blogs and news feeds, has a local AI model summarize each article and judge it against interests you've written in plain English, and compiles whatever survives into an e-book — with topic tags, a comprehension quiz, and a link back to the source. You'll leave with one you made, tuned to what you actually care about.

You don't need to be able to code. About half the room won't. Two tracks run side by side: if you code, you'll be in the Python; if you don't, you'll be shaping the agent's judgment in plain English through a simple app. Same build, same result — we regroup at the end and compare what each track made.

One thing to do before the day — please don't skip this

The AI model runs on your laptop, which means it has to get onto your laptop first: about 9 GB all told. That's a coffee break on home wifi and a non-starter on shared wifi with everyone doing it at once — so it needs to happen before you arrive.

  1. Download the project: [REPO LINK] → green Code button → Download ZIP → unzip it
  2. Double-click setup.command (Mac — the first time, right-click → Open) or setup.bat (Windows)
  3. Leave it running and walk away. It installs everything, then stops.

That's the whole job. It deliberately doesn't open the app — the first time you actually run the thing, you'll be doing it with the rest of us.

Please get it done by [DATE − 2 DAYS] so there's time to sort out anything that goes wrong.

The pre-workshop form

Ten questions, six of them one-click. Each is here because an answer changes something you'd otherwise discover at 9:05 on the day — how to split the room, who can't install software, whose laptop has no setup script.

Four things to settle before this goes out

All four are quick, and all four are cheaper to fix now than on a Tuesday morning with people watching.

The repo has to be public

The email tells people to hit Download ZIP on GitHub. Your remote is an SSH URL, so I couldn't verify visibility from here — open github.com/valkiii/build_your_agent_workshop in a private window before you send, or the whole instruction dead-ends.

There's no Linux path

The repo ships .command for Mac and .bat for Windows — nothing else. A Linux participant has no double-click route and no written fallback. Question 4 catches them; a short setup.sh or a manual crib sheet would close it properly.

The samples folder spoils the ending

samples/ ships a finished e-book, a sample podcast and a saved run — facilitator backup, but it's in the ZIP everyone downloads. If the reveal matters to you, pull it from the participant copy and keep it on your own machine.

Some people won't do it

Assume two or three arrive un-set-up whatever the email says. The graceful answer is to pair them with a neighbour for the morning — worth deciding now, because the alternative is improvising it at 9:05.

Timings match the deck's timetable: 9:00 start, 12:20 finish, tracks split at 9:50.
